01
The Lie You're Believing The belief keeping the gap open

“I just need one more good launch to get back on track.”

The belief that the next spike will solve the infrastructure problem is the trap. It won't. A great launch month is not a business model. It gives you breathing room and dopamine, then the quiet comes back harder because now you've proven you can do it, and the baseline still doesn't reflect that.

02
The Pattern Underneath What's really driving it
Your income pattern is a symptom of a self-worth pattern.

The feast and famine cycle isn't a marketing problem. It's a story your nervous system is running. The belief that visibility has to be earned in concentrated bursts, that you can't just 'be available' in the market without a big moment to justify it. Daily selling feels exposing. Launches feel safer because there's an event to hide behind.

03
Your Next Move The specific action to take this week
Sell something small every single week for the next 8 weeks. No launch required.
✶
Identify your lowest-barrier offer.
What can someone buy from you without getting on a call, attending a webinar, or waiting for a launch window? That is your retention tool. Price it to be a no-brainer, and promote it weekly.
✶
Create a minimum viable visibility standard.
Define what consistent looks like for you, not what it looks like at launch capacity. Two emails a week. Three pieces of content. One direct outreach. Small, non-negotiable, recurring.
✶
Map your last 12 months of revenue against your launch calendar.
You'll see it immediately. The spikes and the silence. That visual is your business case for building a baseline. Keep it somewhere visible.
✦ The Spiker Reality Check ✦
Here's what I need you to know about your pattern.

You'll read this, recognise the pattern completely, and decide that the solution is to plan a better, more strategic launch next quarter. That's the Spiker's version of avoidance. It looks like ambition. The real work is in the unsexy middle: showing up in the market on a Tuesday when there's no campaign live, no deadline, no reason except that it's what consistent businesses do.

The gap between where you are and where your revenue should be is not a launch gap. It's a baseline gap. You've built the ceiling. You haven't built the floor. And until you do, every big month is just buying you time before the next quiet one.

⊙ The Real Cost ⊙

Each feast-and-famine cycle costs you between $15K and $40K per year in revenue that evaporates in the quiet months, revenue that a consistent baseline would have captured. That's the difference between occasional income and a six-figure year that actually holds.

The Spiker doesn't have a strategy problem. She has a baseline problem she's learned to outsprint with launches. The next version of you doesn't need a bigger launch. She needs a floor that catches her between them.
